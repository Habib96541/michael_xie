IMPLEMENTED

UI: Redesigned desktop Home as a split composition with a large existing logo, concise trial identity, gold wallet display, segmented difficulty rail and a clear primary Play action. Portrait uses a compact single-column layout. All screens use a consistent charcoal panel, thin border, small teal accent, bold hierarchy, restrained pink actions and outlined secondary controls. Hover/pressed/focused/disabled colors transition over 0.09 seconds. Safe-step feedback fades briefly; reduced effects disables that fade. No new artwork or fonts were downloaded/generated.

Dialogs now render their own opaque card without the previous Home/HUD text underneath. Settings handles remain inside the slider tracks. Results show only the outcome and payout hierarchy rather than competing with the HUD. Large controls remain at least 48 CSS pixels at 360x800. Choice controls were raised to clear the larger 44-pixel browser fullscreen control. Loading presentation uses the original logo, a restrained radial background and a thin progress track.

A prototype entrance fade could leave dialog content transparent; it was removed. Final browser checks include foreground-text pixel assertions as well as normal input checks. The delivered dialogs appear immediately.

Lighting: Cool directional key at intensity 1.15, teal directional fill at 0.55 and warm rim at 0.45; slate ambient light and exponential-squared fog at density 0.012. Scene-only curtain material overrides reduce saturation while retaining supplied textures. Glass keeps its transparent teal surface and bright edge framing; sharp specular highlights were disabled after compiled-browser screenshots exposed a washed-out reflection. All left/right panels share the same material. No lighting decision depends on safety.

The dedicated Web forward renderer remains unchanged. No HDR, SSAO, deferred rendering, realtime shadow maps, expensive post-processing or replacement assets were introduced. Supplied PC/Mobile pipeline assets and original imported materials remain unchanged. The decorative arena floor was lowered to world Y=-32 to give the descending contestant an open void background.

Falling: The valid fragile landing still resolves in RunController, disables the contacted panel support and triggers one break effect/sound. The contestant immediately descends at a low velocity while developing a startled, off-balance pose over 0.18 seconds. Gravity then takes over while the shoulder/hip rig blends into raised arms, bent asymmetric legs and a tilted torso over the next 0.5 seconds. This is an actual 3D procedural pose animation with Rigidbody descent, not a billboard or an expensive ragdoll.

A fixed oblique camera on the broken-panel side looks back at the gap and lower body space. It does not chase the character into the void. The results delay for falling is now a separate FallResultDelay=2.6 seconds in Sprint1.asset; win/timeout timing remains unchanged. The fall notification sits beneath the HUD rather than across the descending body. Camera framing was iterated against actual frame captures to avoid departure-platform and balcony obstruction.

Pause is now allowed during the terminal Falling presentation. It freezes pose time, Rigidbody motion, fragments, camera and results delay. Resume continues the same fall. Retry/Home resets the contestant's position, visual rotation, arm/leg pose, kinematic state and fall clock; pooled fragments are explicitly cleared. Animation has no authority over safety, coins or terminal outcomes. Timeout remains distinct and does not trigger this fall.
