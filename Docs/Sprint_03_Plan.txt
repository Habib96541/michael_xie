Sprint 03 proposal: Flap Dash visual feedback
Status: approved and implemented; see Sprint_03.txt for verification and pending user review. Sprint 02 understanding answers remain pending.

Why
The controls feel smooth and responsive to the user, but the current static shapes and instant UI transitions provide little animated feedback. Teach the separation of presentation from gameplay: animate visual children and UI without changing the player's physics or collision shape.

Scope
Only the independent Flap Dash project. Games 27-30 remain planned.
Keep the current movement tuning, pipe sequence, scoring, collision rules, and 60-second limit.

Asset requirement supplied by the user
- Externally sourced game content must be verified CC0/public domain. Free or royalty-free labels alone are insufficient.
- Candidate sources and current exceptions are recorded in SprintDocs/Asset_Licenses.txt. Inspect archive licenses and record exact imported files before use.
- Replace the prototype's OFL LiberationSans font and fallback references with a verified public-domain font, such as the creator-dedicated Tuffy family.
- Do not label Unity and its software dependencies as copyright-free; their original licenses still apply.

Proposed visual direction
- A bright neon arcade palette with a dark, readable playfield and glowing accents.
- Flap: animated wing, brief squash/stretch, movement-based tilt, and a short fading trail.
- Pipe passage: a compact particle burst and score pop near the successful action.
- Round transitions: quick panel fade/scale and a brief collision burst; input and restart are never delayed by animation.
- Button interaction: hover and press responses.
- An effects-intensity slider on the Ready/Results screen, defaulting to full intensity at the user's request. Reduced intensity and zero intensity suppress decorative effects while retaining essential gameplay information.
- Keep particles and trails short and outside important text and upcoming openings. Avoid repetitive full-screen flashing. Validate the style with intended players rather than claim an ADHD-specific benefit.

Implementation limits
- Use direct Inspector references and small animation scripts. No extra animation package or shared game framework.
- Change the player's visual child transforms, not its Rigidbody2D or collider transform.
- Particle feedback cannot award points or change hitboxes, timing windows, or input handling.
- Clear transient effects on restart. Repeated rounds must not accumulate objects or listeners.
- Presentation settings do not alter the challenge or score. They remain within the current app session; persistent settings are a later save/load topic.

Done condition
- Flaps, successful passages, button actions, and results have visible, coherent animated feedback.
- Full, reduced, and zero intensity remain playable and readable in portrait view.
- The existing 12 behavior checks still pass; add focused checks for unchanged physics/collider transforms, intensity controls, immediate input/restart, and effect cleanup.
- Inspect animated sequences in Unity and rendered portrait states. Static screenshots alone do not prove animation quality.
- No compilation errors or obsolete API warnings. Inspect repeated-round behavior and obvious particle/frame-time problems.
- User evaluates the new style and explains how presentation stays separate from gameplay.
- Imported content has verified CC0/public-domain provenance and a project-owned license register. Audit remaining default font/fallback assets before claiming compliance.
